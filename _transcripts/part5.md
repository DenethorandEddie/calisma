### 1.2.3.1. Altitude Display Limits for RVSM Operations

Standby altimeters do not meet altimeter accuracy requirements of RVSM airspace.

The maximum allowable in-flight difference between Captain and First Officer altitude displays for RVSM operations is 200 feet.

The maximum allowable on-the-ground altitude display differences for RVSM operations are:

| Field Elevation | Max Difference Between Captain & F/O | Max Difference Between Captain or F/O & Field Elevation |
|---|---|---|
| Sea Level to 5,000 feet | 50 feet | 75 feet |
| 5,001 to 10,000 feet | 60 feet | 75 feet |

### 1.2.4.3. Types of Airplane Operation

The airplane is approved for the following kinds of operation, both day and night, when the required equipment is installed and approved in accordance with the applicable Joint Aviation Regulations (for dispatch with certain items of equipment inoperative or missing, see MEL 737-800/-800W/-8(MAX)):

- Visual Flight (VFR)
- Instrument Flight (IFR)
- Required Navigation Performance (RNP)
- Icing Conditions
- Night Flight
- Over Water Operation
- Automatic Coupled Approach or Automatic Landing at Cat II Weather Minima (dual channel only).
- Automatic Coupled Approach or Automatic Landing at Cat III Weather Minima (dual channel only). *(change bar in left margin)*
- Reduced Vertical Separation (RVSM) Operation.

### 1.2.4.6. Limitations on Wet or Contaminated Runways

| Contamination | Maximum Depth |
|---|---|
| Dry snow | 101.6 mm |
| Standing water, slush or wet snow | 12.7 mm |

- The use of reduced thrust based on the assumed temperature method is not allowed on contaminated / slippery runways.
- Take off with light coatings of frost up to 3 mm in thickness on lower wing surface due to cold fuel, is permissible, however, all leading edge devices, all control surfaces, tab surfaces, upper wing surfaces and balance panel cavities must be free of snow or ice. Do not retract flaps beyond 15, when runways and taxiways are contaminated until inspection shows them free of ice.
- When operating the wing flaps during low temperatures the flap position indicator and leading edge devices annunciator panel must be closely observed for positive movement. If the flaps should stop, the flap lever must be placed immediately in the same position as indicated.

\* Refer to OM-A 8.3 - Operation on Runways with Reduced Runway Condition Code *(change bar)*

> **NOTE**
> Sideslip (Wing Low) Landing is not authorized for SunExpress.

1. The crosswind component (including gust) for takeoff and landing shall not exceed the values specified above.
2. For takeoff in gusty and strong crosswind conditions, maximum takeoff thrust is recommended.
3. The rolling takeoff procedure is strongly advised when crosswind exceeds 20 knots.
4. Takeoff on untreated snow should only be attempted when no melting is present.
5. Minimum runway width for regular SunExpress operation is 30m (100ft)
6. Landing on untreated snow should only be attempted when no melting is present.
7. During Landing reduce crosswind guidelines by 5 knots on wet or contaminated runways whenever asymmetric reverse thrust is used. This condition is not checked by landing performance tools.

> **NOTE**
> Winds measured at 10m (33 ft) tower height.

> **CAUTION**
> With braking action less than medium, neither tailwind landings nor tailwind takeoffs shall be performed.

> **CAUTION**
> The crosswind values above are far more conservative than Boeing values.
