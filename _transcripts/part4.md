### 3.10. Engine Fire or Engine Severe Damage or Engine Separation during Takeoff
*(heading in orange)*

Non-Normal checklist use starts when the airplane path and configurations are correctly established. Try not to perform checklists during high workloads. Outside the area of responsibility of the PF in most cases the PF does not need to respond to the checklist items, but it is important that the workload allows both pilots to verify the progress of the checklist. The crew may need to stop a checklist for a short time to do other tasks. The commands "Hold checklist" or "Holding checklist" or "Continue checklist" or "Continuing checklist" can be given by the PF or PM as the situation demands. Do not confuse the holding of a checklist with the Deferred Items of some non-normal checklists.

Checklists are called by PF and read by PM (Non-Normal checklists as read and do checklists, challenge- response- action- response) and answered in the area of responsibility.

1. The PF calls for the NNC at safe altitude and if time permits (in this example, "Engine Fire Checklist" from the Engine Fire, Engine Severe Damage or Separation NNC). Don't jump ahead on checklist steps, i.e. hold ("Holding checklist") at APU start & On Bus until APU is on Bus.

### 3.12.1. Multiple Failure Situations
*(heading in orange)*

In case of a multiple failure situation, the Flight Crew has to determine if a Non-Normal Checklist containing Deferred Items has priority.

Only if multiple Checklists containing Deferred Items are used, the title of the Checklist referred to must be added, e.g. "ONE ENGINE INOPERATIVE LANDING DEFERRED DESCENT CHECKLIST."

### 3.17. Emergency Landing / Ditching
*(heading in orange)*

If, due to an emergency during a flight, the safe outcome of a landing cannot be assured, the Cabin Crew must be informed by the Captain that the command "BRACE FOR IMPACT, BRACE FOR IMPACT" can be expected from the flight deck over the PA system during final approach. The timing of this announcement should take into consideration the time required to assume the brace position, and the ability of passengers to maintain the brace position for the duration of the time remaining until landing or impact.

> **CAUTION** *(orange-bordered caution box)*
>
> If due to an emergency during a flight the safe outcome of a landing cannot be assured, the Cabin Crew must be informed by the Captain that the command - Brace for Impact - can be expected from the flight deck.

<u>During Ditching:</u>

At 500 feet, advise the Cabin Crew that ditching is imminent.

At 50 feet, announce "BRACE FOR IMPACT, BRACE FOR IMPACT."
